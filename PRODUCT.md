# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is hiring managers and technical interviewers evaluating Raphael for senior full-stack, applied-AI, or integration-heavy roles. The secondary audience is founders and selective clients considering part-time product work. Visitors typically arrive with limited time and need to assess role fit, technical depth, judgment, and evidence quickly.

## Product Purpose

The portfolio is a career-proof hub designed to earn a qualified interview. It should make Raphael's current identity, specialty, credibility, and strongest evidence understandable within ten seconds, then support deeper evaluation through professional experience, selected work, a case study, and the résumé.

## Positioning

Raphael builds production AI workflows, integration-heavy platforms, and full-stack products across web and mobile.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: View experience.
- Secondary evaluation path: Continue from professional experience into one flagship case study and two supporting product proofs.
- Utility action: Download résumé.
- The line a visitor remembers after ten seconds: Raphael builds production AI workflows, integration-heavy platforms, and full-stack products across web and mobile.
- Belief ladder: Raphael is a current senior engineer; his specialty matches production AI, integrations, and full-stack delivery; his professional experience demonstrates credible technical depth; his public work proves end-to-end ownership and engineering judgment; he is worth inviting to a qualified interview.
- Proof on hand: verified professional experience led by VISEO and HustleWing; reviewed public work for Ample News, KudosCourts, and CravingsPH; a public résumé; and credible awards and hackathon recognition. No testimonials or employer logos are currently available.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, and credible. The experience should leave hiring managers confident in Raphael's judgment and technical depth. Linear is a reference for calm hierarchy, restrained secondary UI, and deliberate information density. Lee Robinson is a reference for immediate identity and current-role clarity. These references guide principles rather than prescribe a visual style.

## Anti-references

The portfolio should not resemble a SaaS dashboard, a heavily animated showcase, or a generic developer template assembled from metric cards, repeated uppercase labels, and technology inventories. It should avoid decorative complexity that competes with evidence.

## Design Principles

1. **Make the hiring case immediately.** Resolve identity, specialty, credibility, and the next action within the opening viewport.
2. **Spend attention on evidence.** Let professional experience, inspectable work, ownership, constraints, and consequential decisions carry more weight than decorative UI or broad capability claims.
3. **Reveal depth progressively.** Keep the homepage fast to scan and use case studies for detailed evaluation instead of placing exhaustive history on one page.
4. **Practice disclosure-safe precision.** Every claim must remain evidence-backed, current, and appropriate for public release; confidentiality is part of professional credibility.
5. **Keep calm structure accessible.** Hierarchy, contrast, spacing, and direct language should create confidence without relying on animation, low-contrast metadata, or interaction-dependent visibility.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, meaningful text contrast in light and dark themes, visible focus states, reduced-motion preferences, and comfortable touch targets. Essential content must remain visible without waiting for JavaScript-triggered entrance effects. Color and motion may reinforce hierarchy but must never carry meaning alone.
