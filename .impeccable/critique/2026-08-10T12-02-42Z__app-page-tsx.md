## Anti-pattern verdict

**AI-slop check: pass, with reservations.** The portfolio is rescued by unusually specific ownership, boundary, and ethics language. The art direction itself is less ownable: Inter, a centered 740px rail, sky-blue accent, mono uppercase metadata, status pills, six-tag project rows, and repeated project templates create a familiar generated-portfolio grammar. The content is distinct; the presentation is not yet equally distinctive.

The static detector ran once and returned **11 advisories under one rule (`design-system-font-size`)**. Nine are consistent with the documented system: 36/46px hero display type and seven 10/11px label/metadata values. Two are credible drift signals: the **13px evidence caption** and **28px section heading** are outside the documented steps.

The browser overlay executed successfully in an isolated headless session and produced 12 runtime signals: one `layout-transition`, six `tiny-text`, three `all-caps-body`, and two `overused-font`. The tiny-text/font alerts mostly overlap intentional label tokens, but the long uppercase project metadata and `max-height` menu animation deserve human review. The overlay was confirmed by captured evidence; there is no persistent `[Human]` browser tab. The page produced **zero console errors or warnings**.

## Overall impression

The opening answers the hiring manager’s first questions quickly: who Raphael is, what he does, where he works, and where to inspect proof. The page then loses momentum. Three project entries each expand into problem, ownership, decision, boundary, action links, a diagram, caption, and six technology tags. That is case-study depth presented before the reader has chosen a case study. The result is credible but effortful: the site demonstrates rigor while making the evaluator do too much synthesis.

The visual system is calm, responsive, and mostly accessible, but its strongest qualities are restraint and copy—not a memorable visual point of view. The next pass should protect the clarity while making proof easier to compare and the identity harder to confuse with another polished engineering portfolio.

## Cognitive load

**Moderate: 3 of 8 checks fail.**

- **Chunking fails in Selected Work.** Each entry exposes more than four conceptual layers before the next project begins.
- **Visual hierarchy weakens within project entries.** Problem, ownership, decision, boundary, diagrams, actions, and tags compete at similar emphasis, so the key hiring signal is not obvious at a glance.
- **Progressive disclosure fails.** The homepage reproduces much of the case-study structure instead of giving a compact hiring brief and letting the detail page carry the full proof.
- The opening desktop viewport presents roughly eleven interactive targets across navigation, calls to action, and social links. They are grouped coherently, but the number dilutes the “one obvious next action” principle.

## Emotional journey

- **Arrival — strong.** Identity, specialty, current role, and proof routes are immediate.
- **Experience — reassuring.** The chronology and current-role status build confidence without overselling.
- **Selected Work — confidence valley.** Density slows comparison; the first showcased project is completed/discontinued and its detail link is broken.

## What’s working

1. **The hiring case is explicit.** The hero names the role and present context instead of opening with a vague personal manifesto.
2. **Evidence is precise and ethically scoped.** Ownership, decisions, constraints, and sanitized artifacts communicate senior judgment without pretending confidential outcomes are public.
3. **The accessibility foundation is thoughtful.** Semantic tokens, visible focus styling, reduced-motion support, responsive composition, and the absence of horizontal overflow give the overhaul a solid base.
4. **The page renders cleanly at both audited sizes.** At 1440×1000 the content rail is about 740px; at 390×844 the page remains within the viewport and the hero wraps without clipping.

## Priority issues

### 1. P1 — Ample News presents a broken proof path

- **Location:** First item in Selected Work; link to `/work/ample-news`.
- **Problem:** The interface offers the project as a completed case study, but the route returns 404. The other sampled local routes and evidence assets return 200.
- **Why it matters:** This is a direct trust failure at the moment a hiring manager tries to verify the strongest claim. Because it is the first project, it colors the reliability of every later artifact.
- **Fix:** Restore the case-study route or remove/replace the action until it exists. Add a build-time or test-time internal-link integrity check so portfolio proof cannot ship as a dead end.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 2. P1 — Closed mobile navigation leaves invisible links focusable

- **Location:** `components/portfolio/navbar.tsx`; collapsed menu rendered with `max-h-0` and `opacity-0`.
- **Problem:** The closed menu remains in the DOM with anchors that can still receive keyboard focus. The animation hides pixels, not interaction.
- **Why it matters:** Keyboard and switch users can tab into controls they cannot see, lose their place, and encounter a navigation state that does not match the visual interface.
- **Fix:** Unmount the closed panel or apply a correctly supported inert/hidden pattern. Expose `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`, support Escape, return focus to the trigger, and validate the complete keyboard loop.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### 3. P2 — Homepage project previews duplicate case-study depth

- **Location:** `components/portfolio/project-item.tsx`; all three Selected Work entries.
- **Problem:** Every preview repeats problem, ownership, decision, boundary, actions, evidence figure, caption, and six technology tags. The mobile document reaches about 7,313px, with Selected Work carrying most of the length.
- **Why it matters:** A hiring manager cannot compare impact and scope quickly. Strong evidence becomes visually equal-weight, so credibility turns into reading burden.
- **Fix:** Limit each homepage project to roughly four layers: context/outcome, owned decision, one proof artifact, and one primary action. Move full boundary notes, detailed captions, and complete technology inventories into the case studies. Make the three projects deliberately comparable.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### 4. P2 — Evidence diagrams are not adapted for mobile or dark mode

- **Location:** `components/portfolio/evidence-figure.tsx` and `public/work/*/*.svg`.
- **Problem:** Approximately 1000px-wide diagrams are reduced to about 342px on mobile, leaving source labels around 5.5–8.6px in the captured page. The figure surface is hard-coded to `#f6f6f3`, so it behaves as a light island in the dark theme.
- **Why it matters:** The diagrams are supposed to be the proof. When labels become unreadable or visually disappear, the user sees decorative boxes instead of evidence; dark-mode glare also breaks the documented theme system.
- **Fix:** Create mobile-specific diagram compositions or responsive crops with a minimum rendered label size around 12–14px. Replace hard-coded light colors with semantic figure tokens and verify contrast in both SSR themes.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### 5. P2 — The close is precise but undercommitted as a hiring action

- **Location:** Hero supporting copy and Contact section.
- **Problem:** The page explains how Raphael works but does not clearly state the target role, availability, preferred engagement, or what a qualified visitor should do next. “The difficult middle” is memorable internally but ambiguous externally.
- **Why it matters:** The portfolio earns interest without converting it into a low-friction, relevant interview invitation. Recruiters must infer whether an outreach is timely or appropriate.
- **Fix:** State the desired role/engagement and availability with honest specificity. Translate the metaphor into one concrete buyer-facing outcome, then make email the singular primary close while keeping secondary networks subordinate.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Minor observations

- Recognition is a meaningful hiring signal but is absent from primary navigation.
- The `RM` home mark is less recognizable to a first-time visitor than Raphael’s surname.
- `rethndr.com` appears without explaining whether it is a studio, alias, or separate product.
- `border-l-2` on employer projects is a side-stripe accent, explicitly disallowed by the current Impeccable design rules; use a full boundary, spacing, icon, or tonal surface instead.
- The 13px evidence caption and 28px section heading should either become documented tokens or return to the established type steps.
- Repeated status pills, mono labels, and six-tag technology rows create the strongest “template” tells.
- A skip link would improve repeated-navigation bypass for keyboard users.
- Several visible mobile links are under 44px in one dimension (`RM`, award links, `rethndr.com`). Inline text links are not automatically failures, but the most important actions should provide a larger hit area.
